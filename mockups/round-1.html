A

Lyric Miner

Yomitan inside your player. Tap any lyric word, look it up, mine it. Cards keep the lyric line and a clip of the song, so you review by hearing the word sung.
listen → glance → tap word → mine → later: review deck with the song clip
Feel
Anki and Yomitan, built around music.
Good
Lowest build cost; yomitan-core does most of it
Never interrupts listening
Proven loop (sentence mining)
Bad
Barely gamified; progress is just a card count
Reviewing is a separate chore, away from the music
No sense of "I understand this song now"
B

Song Mastery

Every song gets a score: the share of its words you know. Your library becomes a progress map. The app points you at songs you almost understand, and learning their last few words takes them to 100%.
see "92%, 4 words left" → learn those 4 (30s) → listen with unknowns marked → song reaches 100% → artist and album progress goes up
Feel
Completionist. Like LingQ or jpdb, but your music library is the course.
Good
Answers "do I understand this song yet?" directly
Small, achievable targets each time (learn the next most useful word)
Gamifies through your library and artists, not a mascot
Bad
Needs a large known-word list on day 1, or every song shows 30%
Word coverage ignores grammar, wordplay, and poetic phrasing
Depends on how well yomitan-core splits the lyrics into words
C

Lyric Rhythm

An optional play mode timed to the synced lyrics. A due word is blanked out of the next line, and you pick the answer before it's sung. Combos, scores, and personal bests per song.
toggle Play mode → next line shows with a blank → answer before the line is sung → combo → end-of-song results update your SRS
Feel
Rhythm game meets vocab quiz. The most fun, and the most demanding.
Good
Best use of synced lyrics; nobody else does this
You review a word as you hear it sung
Easy to make social later (scores per song)
Bad
Demands eyes on screen; not "look down if you feel like it"
Breaks completely when lyric timing is off
Recognition-only (multiple choice) is weak practice for N2
D

Study Radio

Your SRS schedule picks the music. Autoplay builds a station from songs that contain your due words. When a due word is about to be sung, the lyrics view and the lock screen show it, and one tap grades it. Doing nothing is fine.
hit play station → songs are picked for their due words → due word coming up → glance, tap knew / missed (or ignore) → queue adjusts
Feel
A radio that teaches you. The most passive option.
Good
Matches "look down if you feel like it" best
Puts substreamer's autoplay engine to new use
You review a word as you hear it sung, with no extra effort
Bad
Takes away control over what plays (fine for a station, bad all the time)
Self-graded "knew it" gets sloppy
Live Activity with buttons is native Swift work
Needs words to be mined first (A or B supplies them)
E

Line Decoder

The unit is a lyric line, not a word. Rate each line as you hear it: got it, fuzzy, or lost. Lines you didn't get open into a breakdown (words, grammar, translation) and become sentence cards. A song is decoded when you get every line.
listen → tap a line you didn't get → breakdown with grammar → save line → review the line with its clip → song decoded
Feel
A study companion for working through songs, line by line.
Good
Matches the real N3/N2 gap: grammar, phrasing, and subtext
"Decoded" is an honest measure of understanding
Line interpretations are something people would want to share (phase 2)
Bad
Grammar notes and translations need an LLM or some other source
Breakdowns are heavier reading; less glanceable
Rating every line gets tedious; needs good defaults

My pick: B as the core, A as how you add words, D later as a station mode. Song coverage is the scoreboard and decides what's worth learning next. Tap-to-look-up is how you mark words known or add them. The radio comes after that, once there's a deck worth scheduling. C and E can wait; both make sense as modes added on top of B.
