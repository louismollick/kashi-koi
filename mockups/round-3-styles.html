Round 3: visual styles

Six directions for the game screens, all showing the same two moments: Meaning Match in progress (line 4 is being sung, you pick its meaning) and the results screen, where you choose which missed lines go to review. Walk mode would follow the same style, but calmer.
← Round 3 loop 1 · Doodle 2 · Matsuri Drum 3 · Pixel JRPG 4 · Manga 5 · Lo-fi Room 6 · Purikura
1

Doodle (skribbl.io)

Blue doodle wallpaper, white panels, marker font, googly-eyed blob avatars. Your opponent is a ghost of your previous best run; later, friends' runs. Guesses scroll by in the chat log like skribbl.
Vibe
Party game, scrappy, friendly. Very lo-fi.
Good
Ghost opponent makes solo runs feel social; becomes real friends later
Cheap to build: flat panels, no art assets
Risk
Little that's Japanese about it; the marker font does the work
2

Matsuri Drum (Taiko)

Festival orange, wave-pattern floor, chunky outlined text, a drum mascot. Each lyric line is a note sliding into the hit circle; when it lands, the line is being sung. Answers are red, blue, and yellow drum buttons. Judgements use Taiko's 良 / 可 / 不可.
Vibe
Arcade at a summer festival. Loud, happy, very Japanese.
Good
The note lane makes timing obvious: you see the next line coming
良/可/不可 teaches a bit of Japanese for free
Risk
Busiest to build (lane animation, mascot art)
Easy to end up looking like a Taiko knockoff
3

Pixel JRPG

Each line is a monster. Pick the right meaning from the command menu to hit it before the line ends. A song is a dungeon, and your review list is the quest log. Uses the DotGothic16 pixel font, which covers kanji.
Vibe
Dragon Quest on a Game Boy. Nostalgic, funny, low effort to look good.
Good
Gives the meta layer a theme for free: dungeons, EXP, quest log
Pixel art hides how cheap the art is
Risk
Long English choices are cramped in pixel type
Combat theme sits oddly with sad ballads
4

Manga

Black ink on white paper. Screentone, speed lines, onomatopoeia. Answers are speech bubbles, a correct pick gets a red 正解 stamp, and hits make a big ドン. The game in black and white, closest to your usual taste but still playful.
Vibe
Shonen tension in monochrome. Anime-adjacent without being cute.
Good
Stays black and white, so it fits your dark-mode taste (an inverted version is easy)
Effects are pure CSS: tone, speed lines, stamps
Risk
White paper by default; the black-paper version needs testing
Can look busy around dense Japanese
5

Lo-fi Room

The "lofi hip hop radio" anime-girl room. Dusk window with rain, city lights, a cassette for the timer, sticky notes for answers. Calmer game feel; probably the best match for Walk mode, and soft enough for Sit.
Vibe
Cozy, rainy, nighttime study. Least "game", most "chill".
Good
Works for both modes; Walk mode could just be the room with the lyrics
Room could change with time of day or the song's album art colors
Risk
Rhythm-game energy is low; combo feedback feels out of place
6

Purikura

Photo-booth stickers. Pastel holographic gradient, sparkles, a round hand-drawn font (Hachi Maru Pop), sticker-outlined buttons. Results look like a printed sticker sheet. The most "kawaii" option.
Vibe
Y2K Harajuku. Cute, loud, unserious.
Good
Very distinctive; nothing else looks like this
Sticker metaphor could extend to collecting lines
Risk
The furthest from your taste; can wear thin
